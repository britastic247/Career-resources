Chronological (Reverse-Chronological) Resumes

This is the most common format. It's the standard most recruiters expect. It lists your jobs starting with the most recent one and moving back in time.

Best for:

  • Consistently employed
  • Most jobs lasted at least 6 months
  • Only minor gaps between jobs
  • Returning to work after 3 years or less, with steady work before that

Key focus:

Career growth, job titles, and dates.

Pros:

  • Very easy for recruiters to skim
  • Works very well with Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS), the software that sorts resumes

Cons:

  • Makes employment gaps easy to see
  • Repetitive if you did the same tasks at several jobs

Hybrid (Skills-Based) Resumes

A hybrid resume focuses on what you can do, not where and when you worked. It groups your experience under skill themes, like "Customer Service" or "Equipment Operation," instead of job titles. Your work history is still listed, but shorter.

Best for:

  • Limited work experience
  • Lots of employment gaps
  • Short jobs
  • Mostly project-based work, like contractors, skilled trades, or self-employed

Key focus:

Your skills and the experience you can bring to a new job.

Pros:

  • Draws attention away from gaps or short jobs
  • Makes sense of project-based work

Cons:

  • Can confuse Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS), which are built for chronological resumes
