Career Exploration Resources

Free tools to help you explore, compare, and choose a career.

5 Steps to Choosing a Career

  1. Get clear on your goals. Let yourself dream first. Think about lifestyle, family, travel, health, and money. These all affect the pay and location you'll need.
  2. List what you have to work with. Your strengths, financial support, local training programs, and your target job zone (how much school or training you're willing to do).
  3. Explore your interests and jobs. Use CA Career Zone, O*NET, or My Next Move to match your interests, values, and income needs to real jobs.
  4. Narrow it down. Keep a simple list or spreadsheet of jobs you're thinking about, including ones you ruled out and why. Pick your top 3 and compare the costs and benefits.
  5. Make a realistic plan and stick to it. Stay motivated. If the plan isn't working, check in with a case manager or career professional.

Career Exploration Tools

  • CA Career Zone (opens in a new tab)

    Take free quizzes about your interests, motivations, and budget. Then see jobs that match. Shows California pay and job growth. Note: this site will be retired soon.

  • California Career Surfer (opens in a new tab)

    A phone app to explore more than 900 jobs. It has a quick interest quiz. A good choice if you don't have a computer.

  • My Next Move (opens in a new tab)

    Take a 5 to 10 minute interest quiz. Then search or browse careers by keyword, industry, or job zone.

  • Local Job Projections (opens in a new tab)

    See which jobs and industries are expected to grow near you. Filter by education needed, job, industry, and area.

  • O*NET Job Explorer (opens in a new tab)

    The most detailed source for researching one job: what the work involves and how to stand out.

  • O*NET Green Topics (opens in a new tab)

    Browse 72 kinds of “green” careers if you're interested in environmental work.

  • Greater Sacramento Pathway Profiles (opens in a new tab)

    Pick an industry to see local job demand, pay, and the skills employers want.

Words to Know

These terms show up in the tools.

Job Zone (1 to 5)
How much education, training, and experience a job usually needs. 1 means little or none (like a high school diploma). 5 means a lot (like a graduate degree). A higher zone doesn't always mean higher pay. Some Job Zone 3 jobs pay more than Job Zone 5 jobs.
Career Cluster
A group of jobs that use similar skills, like Health Sciences, IT, or Manufacturing. Helpful for finding related jobs if you want to keep your options open.
RIASEC
An interest quiz with 6 types: Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, and Conventional. Your top 3 letters are your "code." Tools use your code to match you with jobs.
